# Research Origin

Phonic Drive began as a personal research question rather than as a claim of established mechanism.

The originating observation was that several sound compositions and recurring musical patterns were repeatedly associated with a strong hair-raising response (piloerection) in different parts of the body. After grouping tracks that appeared to produce similar responses, the exploration expanded through interoception, meditation, and deliberate deep listening.

During that process, some listening sessions included a pronounced subjective sense of motion, spatial expansion, or directional change while the body remained physically stationary. The name **Phonic Drive** came from that phenomenological description: sound seemed to produce an experience of movement without literal movement.

The project therefore asks a narrower and more testable question than the original intuition:

> Can time-varying acoustic structure be measured independently, subjective embodied/spatial experience be recorded independently, and the two timelines later be compared without assuming in advance that one causes the other?

This question led to the current architecture:

```text
A(t) = measured acoustic state
P(t) = reported phenomenological state
K(t) = optional future behavioral telemetry
B(t) = optional future biosensor telemetry
```

The streams remain separate so that later analysis can examine temporal relationships, repeated patterns, and failures to correspond without silently converting subjective experience into an acoustic or physiological measurement.

## Research posture

Phonic Drive is an instrument for structured observation and comparison. It does not require the originating hypothesis to be correct.

A useful result may be:

- a repeatable association,
- a context-dependent association,
- different experiential classes with different acoustic correlates,
- no stable relationship,
- or evidence that an earlier interpretation should be revised.

The instrument should remain useful under all of those outcomes.

## Public-facing principle

The autobiographical origin explains why the instrument exists. The software, however, should make its claims from inspectable records, explicit transformations, repeatable analyses, and bounded conclusions rather than from the origin story itself.
